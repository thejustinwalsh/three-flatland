# Banned vocabulary and structures

Loaded by `technical-writing`. Consolidated from four public anti-slop rule sets, filtered for
technical documentation. Subtractive only: nothing here overrides a Google rule.

Sources: [avoid-ai-writing](https://github.com/conorbronsdon/avoid-ai-writing),
[stop-slop](https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop),
[anti-ai-slop-writing](https://github.com/jalaalrd/anti-ai-slop-writing),
[no_ai_slop_writing_rules](https://github.com/realrossmanngroup/no_ai_slop_writing_rules).

## Vocabulary

Replace on sight.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| delve, deep dive, unpack | look at, walk through |
| leverage, utilize, harness | use |
| robust, comprehensive | reliable, complete, thorough |
| seamless, frictionless | smooth, or name what stopped breaking |
| streamline, optimize | speed up, simplify, or give the number |
| crucial, pivotal, paramount | important, or say what breaks without it |
| landscape, realm, ecosystem, space | field, or name the thing |
| intricate, nuanced, multifaceted | name the specific complexity |
| showcase, boast, serve as, feature | show, has, is |
| empower, unlock, elevate, supercharge | let, enable, improve |
| foster, bolster, underscore, garner | build, support, show, collect |
| testament to, at its core | delete, state the fact |
| meticulous, vibrant, groundbreaking | delete, give the detail |
| transformative, game-changing | describe what changed |
| significantly, dramatically, extremely | give the number |

Three or more in one paragraph means rewrite, not edit.

## Openers and filler

Delete: `In today's …`, `It's worth noting that`, `It's important to note`, `Let's dive in`,
`Let's take a look`, `When it comes to`, `At the end of the day`, `In conclusion`, `To summarize`,
`Here's the thing`, `The catch?`, `Without further ado`, `In a nutshell`.

Delete as sentence openers: `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally`, `Interestingly`, `Notably`,
`Importantly`, `Indeed`, `Certainly`, `Absolutely`.

Delete adverb crutches: `just`, `simply`, `really`, `actually`, `literally`, `genuinely`, `truly`,
`honestly`.

## Structures

**Binary contrast.** `It's not X, it's Y` and every variant: `The question isn't X, it's Y`,
`X isn't the problem, Y is`, `not just X but Y`, `stops being X and starts being Y`. State Y.

**Negative listing.** `Not a X. Not a Y. A Z.` State Z.

**Dramatic fragmentation.** `Noun. That's it. That's the thing.` Complete sentences.

**Rhetorical setup.** `What if …?`, `Here's what I mean:`, `Think about it:`, `So why should you
care?` Make the point.

**False agency.** Complaints do not become fixes, decisions do not emerge, data does not tell you
anything, the market does not reward. Name the actor, or use *you*.

**Staccato.** Three or more short declaratives in a row. Connect them with clauses or conjunctions.

**Rule of three.** Three parallel clauses or three-item lists as a default rhythm. Use two or four
unless the content genuinely has three.

**Hedge stacking.** `could potentially`, `may eventually`, `might ultimately`. Pick one hedge.

**Vague attribution.** `studies show`, `experts believe`, `research suggests`. Name the source or cut
the clause.

**Lazy extremes.** `every`, `always`, `never`, `everyone`, `nobody` doing vague work.

## Punctuation

**Em dashes.** Target zero. The list-item form `- **Term** — description` is typography and does not
count.

**Exclamation marks.** None. Google's tone guidance bans them.

**Bold.** One phrase per section at most. Never bold a modifier or a bare number with no subject.

## What was dropped from the corpus, and why

Parts of these rule sets target essays, blog posts, and social media. They actively harm reference
documentation, so they are excluded:

- **"Include friction, doubt, or mess"** and **"let sentences be ugly"** — reference docs are read by
  someone in a hurry looking for a fact. Deliberate roughness costs them time.
- **"Put the reader in the room"** and narrative scene-setting — Google's neutral register governs.
- **Voice calibration to a person** (swearing, slang, humor) — Google bans humor and slang outright
  because they do not translate.
- **"No passive construction, ever"** — Google allows passive in three named cases. Google wins.
- **"No Wh- sentence openers"** — an essay heuristic. Conditions-first phrasing often needs one, and
  Google's own examples use it.
- **Emoji, hashtag, and thread rules** — no applicable surface here.

## Where the corpus and Google conflict

**Sentence length.** Google wants short sentences for clarity and translation. The corpus warns that
stacked short declaratives read as machine staccato. Both hold: write short sentences, vary their
length, and connect related thoughts rather than chaining fragments.

**Contractions.** The corpus mandates them. Google permits them. Use them where they read naturally.
